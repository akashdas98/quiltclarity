## Acceptance Rule

Recruit five quilters who have used an external quilt pattern or a self-drafted cut list. At least four of the five must, without step-by-step facilitation:

1. complete the representative project plan;
2. correctly identify which fabric is fully covered by stock;
3. correctly identify what must be purchased; and
4. locate the cutting plan.

Any repeated inability to distinguish **Pattern says**, **Planned fresh-fabric requirement**, and **Buy now** blocks launch even if four participants otherwise finish.

Do not mark M9 complete until five session records and the signed aggregate result are present in this document or a linked evidence artifact.

## Session Setup

- Use a current production-like static build in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari on the participant's normal computer.
- Start in a new private browser window or clean test profile so another person's saved project cannot appear.
- Begin at `/fabric-cutting-planner/` with no walkthrough.
- Ask the participant to share their screen or sit with a neutral observer. Think-aloud is welcome but not required.
- Do not collect names, pattern files, personal project content, or unrelated browser data in the study record. Assign participant IDs `U01` through `U05`.
- Record browser, viewport/device class, elapsed time, task outcome, observed friction, and the participant's answers. Recording audio/video is unnecessary.

## Participant Brief

Give the participant only the following brief and source sheet. Do not tell them which controls to use or where answers appear.

> You are preparing a two-fabric quilt project. Use QuiltClarity to turn the supplied cut list, fabric on hand, and pattern yardage into a shopping-and-cutting plan. You may enter or paste the cut list in whatever way seems easiest. When you are done, tell me:
>
> 1. which fabric is covered by stock;
> 2. what fabric you need to buy and how much;
> 3. what “Pattern says,” “Planned fresh-fabric requirement,” and “Buy now” mean here; and
> 4. where you would go to follow the cutting plan.

Project facts:

- Units: imperial.
- Fabrics: `Blue` and `Cream`.
- Both fabrics: 42-inch nominal width, 40-inch usable WOF, non-directional, rotation allowed, 5% purchase safety, 1/8-yard purchase increments.
- Blue stock on hand: one custom remnant, 10 × 10 inches.
- Blue pattern reference: 1/4 yard at 44-inch assumed usable WOF.
- Cream stock on hand: none.
- Cream pattern reference: 1/2 yard at 44-inch assumed usable WOF.
- All supplied piece dimensions are cut sizes.

Source cut list:

```text
Fabric	Label	Qty	Width	Height	Size mode
Blue	Blue star centers	2	5	5	Cut
Blue	Blue corner squares	2	5	5	Cut
Cream	Cream background A	4	5	5	Cut
Cream	Cream background B	4	5	5	Cut
Cream	Cream side strips	2	10	3	Cut
Cream	Cream top strips	2	10	3	Cut
```

## Locked Benchmark

The current deterministic domain result for the exact brief is:

- Project status: additional purchase required.
- Blue: all four pieces allocated to the 10 × 10-inch remnant; buy now is zero.
- Cream: all twelve pieces allocated to purchased fabric; raw layout length is 8 inches, and 5% safety plus 1/8-yard upward rounding produces a **1/4-yard** purchase.
- Blue fresh-fabric plan: 1/4 yard; pattern says 1/4 yard.
- Cream fresh-fabric plan: 1/4 yard; pattern says 1/2 yard.
- Both pattern comparisons warn that 44-inch assumed pattern WOF and 40-inch current usable WOF are not like-for-like.
- A valid result exposes stock allocation and purchased-fabric cutting plans in both visual and textual forms.

The benchmark is evaluator-only. Do not disclose it before the participant finishes.

## Neutral Moderator Rules

- Do not point to controls, define labels, fix input, or suggest paste versus table entry while the participant is working.
- If asked what a project fact means, repeat the supplied wording. Do not explain the application's intended model.
- If the participant is blocked, record the point and exact question before providing any help. Any task finished after procedural help is `assisted`, not an unassisted gate pass.
- Technical interruptions unrelated to the product may be restarted and noted.
- After the task, ask the observation questions below even when the participant did not finish.

## Observation Questions

Record behavior first, then ask:

1. Were the supplied dimensions understood as cut sizes? What would the participant expect finished-size mode to do?
2. Was adding the 10 × 10-inch physical remnant clear, or was it mistaken for generic stash yardage?
3. Did the participant choose table entry or paste? Where did either path cause hesitation, correction, or mapping work?
4. In the participant's own words, how do Pattern says, Planned fresh-fabric requirement, and Buy now differ?
5. Which assumptions or warnings did the participant inspect before trusting the result?
6. Could the participant find both the visual plan and its text alternative?
7. What, if anything, would prevent using the shopping list or cutting plan on a real project?

## Interpretation

- `PASS`: at least four eligible participants pass every core item without procedural help, and the pattern/fresh/buy-now concepts do not repeatedly fail.
- `FAIL`: fewer than four pass, or the concept distinction repeatedly fails. Diagnose the common boundary, implement one coherent correction, rerun affected automated gates, and retest the failed usability scope before launch.
- Incomplete recruitment or missing records is `PENDING`, not a pass or a product failure.
