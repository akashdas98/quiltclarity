## Executive conclusion

Quilter has a defensible launch position for its locked workflow: enter arbitrary rectangular piece groups, keep multiple fabrics separate, expose assumptions and warnings, and receive shopping quantities plus optimizer-derived cutting instructions, SVG geometry, and a textual equivalent without an account.

The competitors with broader design canvases, pattern libraries, fabric imagery, cloud sync, community features, or PDF generation serve a larger design workflow. Those capabilities are deliberate Quilter V1 non-goals and are not evidence for scope expansion.

The closest workflow comparison is Gibson Threads. It publicly supports multiple fabrics, block components, yardage, cut maps, and written instructions. Quilter's differentiators are finished-versus-cut input, metric/imperial support, explicit safety and purchase-rounding assumptions, piece-level rotation/orientation controls, local persistence, deterministic warning behavior, and accessible geometry/text projections. Quilter's current weakness is external trust proof: it is not yet on a public domain and therefore has no live ownership, user proof, Search Console history, or monitored public contact channel.

## Decision

The locked core workflow is noticeably more transparent and lower-commitment than the broad design products, and meaningfully more complete than basic one-shape calculators. That is enough differentiation for launch, but “more trustworthy” should remain a product objective rather than an unqualified marketing claim until the public domain, correction channel, operational identity, and real user evidence exist.

No competitor finding justifies adding a design canvas, pattern library, accounts, cloud projects, fabric-image ingestion, community, arbitrary polygon cutting, or PDF generation to V1.

## Differentiation-hardening benchmark questions

The post-overlay benchmark must answer these workflow questions from current first-party evidence rather than feature counts:

1. Can the competitor start from an arbitrary user-entered piece list, or must the user first construct a quilt or pattern inside its ecosystem?
2. Can different piece groups from the same fabric be jointly packed into shared fabric space?
3. Does it exploit leftover width or regions across groups?
4. Can the user specify multiple fabrics independently?
5. Are rotation, direction, usable WOF, finished-versus-cut sizing, and relevant assumptions visible?
6. Does the result show an executable cutting sequence as well as geometry?
7. Can the user understand what the optimization improved?
8. Is the relevant workflow free, partly free, trial-based, one-time paid, or subscription-gated?
9. Is login or account creation required for the relevant workflow?
10. Is the workflow design-first, pattern-first, or piece-list-first?

Decision question: **Is QuiltClarity's arbitrary-piece, joint-planning workflow noticeably easier, more transparent, and more useful for someone who already knows the pieces they need?**

### Finding

The refreshed evidence narrows the claim appropriately: arbitrary cross-group planning itself is **not unique**, because The Quilters Retreat publicly offers optimization across sets and PreQuilt shows mixed generated pieces sharing per-fabric diagrams. QuiltClarity's defensible combination is narrower: direct arbitrary piece-list entry, multiple independently constrained fabrics, explicit finished/cut and orientation assumptions, executable geometry-derived instructions, and a truthful per-fabric measurement of what joint planning changed—without requiring a quilt design or account first.

For a user who already knows the required rectangular pieces, QuiltClarity is clearly lower-setup than the design-first and pattern-first products and more inspectable than the reviewed calculator-suite pages. Against The Quilters Retreat, ease is comparable rather than decisively proven; QuiltClarity is more explicit about constraints and the measured joint-planning effect, while The Quilters Retreat offers a strong direct across-set workflow and downloadable/printable diagrams. This is sufficient launch differentiation, but not evidence for exclusivity claims. No result justifies V1 scope expansion.
