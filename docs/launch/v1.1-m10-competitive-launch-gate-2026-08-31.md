# V1.1 M10 Competitive Launch Gate

**Date:** 2026-08-31  
**QuiltClarity build:** working tree based on `c17435a`; 32-page V1.1 static build  
**Authority:** `docs/v1.1/06_COMPETITIVE_BENCHMARK_GATE.md`

## Decision

**Reason-to-win still present:** YES  
**Website claims verified:** YES  
**LAUNCH GATE:** PASS

QuiltClarity remains meaningfully preferable for the target user who already
has an external rectangular cut list and wants one result that jointly accounts
for exact project-local stock, additional bolt purchase, optional pattern
yardage, and executable cutting geometry. No reviewed competitor completed that
compound job comparably in one workflow.

This PASS closes M10. It does not authorize deployment or claim that the product
is globally launch-ready: the recorded guides overhaul, continuation-document
reconciliation, domain/brand work, and public-origin checks remain separate
pre-launch checkpoints.

## Method and evidence boundary

The benchmark used the current public products and first-party help/listing
material available on 2026-08-31. Public browser tools were inspected in their
rendered forms. iOS/Android-only and paid/trial behavior was scored from current
first-party documentation or store listings where installing or purchasing was
not available. An unavailable behavior is `Unknown`, not `Absent`.

QuiltClarity's cases were executed against the current domain build. The normal
release evidence was also rerun: zero Astro diagnostics, clean lint, 196 passing
tests across 18 files, and a successful 32-page build. The installed Chrome/Edge
release audit passed all 29 indexable routes plus planner/calculator,
persistence, analytics, accessibility, mobile, and print/PDF checks; its
500-piece fallback rendered in 128.6 ms and 139.9 ms respectively. The milestone
completion evidence is also recorded in `docs/architecture/project-status.md`.

## Case C1 — same-fabric mixed requirements

**Fixture:** one 40″-usable fabric; eight 5″ × 5″ cut squares and four 10″ × 3″
cut rectangles; non-directional; 5% purchase safety; 1/8-yard purchase
increment.

**QuiltClarity:** Complete. It jointly placed all 12 pieces in 8″ raw length and
recommended 1/4 yard. The result includes the buy quantity, measured joint-plan
effect, row instructions, SVG geometry, and complete text alternative.

**Closest alternatives:** The Quilters Retreat is Complete for this bounded
fresh-bolt/whole-strip case and is the strongest direct comparison. Gibson
Threads and Quilt Geek are Partial-to-Unknown because mixed calculations and
cut maps are documented, but equivalent cross-group behavior for this exact
fixture was not independently established. QuiltSandwich is Partial because its
documentation explicitly calculates different pieces independently and leaves
cross-piece scrap reconciliation to the user. NiftyFifty and ScrapFit solve
adjacent one-bin problems but not this complete fresh-bolt job.

**Residual work:** none in QuiltClarity. Several alternatives can answer fresh
yardage, but that overlap does not remove the broader reason-to-win.

**Verdict:** Complete; no exclusive C1 claim is made.

## Case C2 — multiple exact stock pieces plus shortfall

**Fixture:** one fabric with a 21″ × 18″ fat quarter, a 10″ × 10″ custom remnant,
and a 40″ × 9″ partial-yard rectangle. Requirements are twenty-four 5″ × 5″
pieces, eight 10″ × 3″ pieces, and four 12″ × 6″ pieces.

**QuiltClarity:** Complete. It kept all three physical-bin identities, allocated
21 of 36 pieces to stock (6 / 4 / 11 by bin), allocated the remaining 15 to a
10″ raw bolt layout, and recommended 3/8 yard after 5% safety and 1/8-yard upward
rounding. All 36 instances were accounted for once, with stock and purchased
plans shown separately inside one result.

**Closest alternatives:** ScrapFit is Partial and stronger than the older
comparison set for real remnant geometry; it can place mixed pieces across
multiple scraps and report unplaced instances, including grain constraints. It
does not turn those unplaced instances into a purchased-bolt plan or combined
execution result. QuiltSandwich is Partial because a scalar starting amount can
remove a fabric from its shopping list, but its per-piece independent yardage
and amount-based stock cannot prove this three-bin fit. NiftyFifty is Partial for
one finite rectangle/one piece size. Other reviewed products are Absent or
Unknown for the compound case.

**Residual work:** alternatives require a second calculator and manual transfer
of unplaced pieces, or area/yardage subtraction that cannot prove geometric fit.

**Verdict:** Complete and materially differentiated. No automatic blocker is
triggered.

## Case C3 — pattern amount, stock, and buy-now

**Fixture:** one 40″-usable fabric, eight 5″ squares, one 10″ × 10″ remnant,
pattern amount 1 yard at 44″ assumed usable WOF, 5% safety, and 1/8-yard purchase
increments.

**QuiltClarity:** Complete. Four pieces were allocated to stock and four to a 5″
raw purchased layout; buy now is 1/4 yard. The independent fresh-fabric scenario
remains separate from stock, the pattern is reported as more than the fresh
recommendation, and the 44″/40″ WOF mismatch is explicit.

**Closest alternatives:** QuiltSandwich is Partial because its project,
starting-amount, and shortage-list features can perform amount subtraction, but
its separate-piece calculation boundary and lack of exact stock bins leave
manual reconciliation. NiftyFifty can start from precuts or produce a shopping
list in separate tools, but no single external-cut-list pattern/fresh/stock/buy
comparison was found. ScrapFit has exact stock but no pattern or purchase layer.
The other reviewed products are Partial or Unknown.

**Residual work:** competitors require manual arithmetic or transfer between
stock, yardage, and pattern outputs.

**Verdict:** Complete; this remains a central compound advantage.

## Case C4 — directional constraint

**Fixture:** directional fabric with rotation disabled, one 12″ × 18″ remnant,
six 7″ × 4″ and six 5″ × 8″ crosswise-constrained pieces.

**QuiltClarity:** Complete. Six pieces were legally placed in the remnant and six
in an 8″ raw purchased layout; buy now is 1/4 yard. Every placement retained the
resolved orientation and bin identity.

**Closest alternatives:** ScrapFit is Complete for directional/grain-aware
stock-only nesting but Partial for the full stock-plus-purchase job. QuiltSandwich
and NiftyFifty expose one-way/directional controls but only within their narrower
independent-piece or one-piece-size models. Quilt Geek advertises directional
fabric-saving layouts, but the exact combined workflow remains Unknown. The
remaining design-first products are Partial or Unknown.

**Residual work:** no manual rotation correction is needed in QuiltClarity;
alternatives still require a separate shortfall/purchase step for this case.

**Verdict:** Complete and at least as trustworthy on the shared directional
function, with automated placement-orientation invariants.

## Case C5 — realistic long cut list

**Fixture:** 20 cut rows across four fabrics, five mixed-size groups per fabric,
80 total pieces.

**QuiltClarity:** Complete. The tabular compiler accepts the complete sheet in
one paste/preview/confirm flow. The executed fixture allocated all 80 pieces,
kept four fabrics independent, and returned four shopping and cutting plans with
no unallocated piece. The larger 500-piece browser fallback remains bounded and
warned by the release audit.

**Closest alternatives:** Gibson Threads and QuiltSandwich require repeated
component/piece entry. Quilt Geek, PreQuilt, QuiltButler, Quiltler, and
NiftyFifty's full fabric planner require block, quilt, supported-pattern, or role
reconstruction that is not present in the source cut list. ScrapFit permits
project JSON but exposes no documented spreadsheet compiler and still lacks the
purchase layer.

**Residual work:** QuiltClarity requires explicit fabric mapping only when a
source fabric label is unmatched. Competitors require substantial row-by-row
entry or visual/design recreation before the target answer.

**Verdict:** Complete; input burden is materially lower for the governed target
state.

## Launch-pass criteria

1. **C1-C3 complete without outside reconciliation:** yes.
2. **Correctness at least as trustworthy on shared functions:** yes. The current
   implementation passes G01-G45, D01-D05, deterministic geometry/accounting/
   orientation properties, the executed C1-C5 fixtures, and browser/print
   projection checks. Competitor marketing claims were not treated as verified
   numerical superiority.
3. **Reasonable external-project input burden:** yes. Direct rows and confirmed
   TSV/CSV paste avoid quilt-design reconstruction; C5 demonstrates the target
   advantage.
4. **Compound combination meaningfully preferable:** yes, specifically for
   quilters starting with a rectangular cut list and measured project-local
   stock.
5. **No newly discovered competitor makes the reason-to-win cosmetic:** yes.
   ScrapFit materially strengthens the remnant-nesting alternative, but it does
   not calculate or execute purchase shortfall or pattern comparison.
6. **Website claims match observed behavior:** yes. Current copy describes the
   compound workflow, local-only/no-account access, editable assumptions, exact
   physical stock, and a practical optimized plan without claiming exclusivity
   or global optimality.

## Best alternative and target boundary

**Best realistic alternative for the complete target job:** QuiltSandwich, if
the user is already in Apple's device ecosystem and accepts manual cross-piece
scrap review plus amount-based rather than exact multi-bin stock reconciliation.

**Best focused alternatives:** The Quilters Retreat for a free fresh-bolt
multi-set calculation; ScrapFit for irregular/defected remnant nesting where no
new purchase plan is needed; Quilt Geek, PreQuilt, or Quiltler when the user
wants to design the quilt digitally and values their broader ecosystems.

**Why choose QuiltClarity:** it starts at the user's existing cut list, accepts
multiple named physical stock rectangles, jointly selects stock and purchased
geometry, keeps pattern comparison independent, and returns one inspectable
shopping-and-cutting result without an account or design recreation.

**Who should choose an alternative instead:** users primarily designing a quilt
visually, managing a broad photo stash, needing irregular polygon/defect-aware
scrap nesting, wanting native mobile/cloud/community features, or working from a
supported named-pattern library.

## Automatic-blocker review

- No competitor was found to handle exact multi-remnant stock, joint external
  cut-list planning, purchase shortfall, and consolidated execution comparably
  in one workflow.
- QuiltClarity requires less reconstruction than the best alternative for its
  governed external-project start state.
- No QuiltClarity output requires manual reconciliation of separate piece-group
  scraps.
- Stock fit is geometry-based, not area/yardage subtraction.
- The benchmark exposed no correctness defect.

Result: none of the automatic launch blockers applies.

## M10 limitations

- Quilt Geek 2.0.0 and QuiltSandwich were not purchased or installed; their
  non-public behavior remains Unknown where first-party material was
  insufficient.
- Competitor output numbers were not used as correctness oracles unless the
  public tool exposed the relevant result directly.
- No external target-user comprehension study occurred. The separate M9
  validation-substitution limitation remains unchanged.
- Competitive conditions can change; this PASS is dated evidence, not a
  permanent exclusivity claim.
